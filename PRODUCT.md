# Product

brand

## Users

People who need to make a raw screenshot look intentional — indie developers and
founders shipping App Store / Play Store screenshot sets, designers and marketers
producing social and landing-page imagery, and creators recording product demos.
They arrive with a flat capture and a deadline, often without a design background,
and they care about owning their work (privacy) and not paying or signing up to get
started. On the marketing site their context is evaluation: "can this make my
screenshots look good in seconds, and can I trust it?"

## Product Purpose

SnapShotPro is a free, browser-based screenshot studio. It turns a raw screenshot
into a finished, on-brand asset — backgrounds, device frames, App Store screenshot
sets, brand kits, generative-AI edits, annotations, liquid glass, interactive tours,
and export to PNG / social sizes / GIF / MP4 — all from one canvas, rendered locally
in the browser with no account required. Success is a visitor who drops in an image
and walks away with something they're proud to publish, trusting that their work
never left their machine. The marketing site's job is to make that promise land:
that beautiful output is the default, that it's free and open, and that privacy is
built in.

## Brand Personality

Confident, crafted, and honest — three words: **assured, editorial, trustworthy.**
The voice is plain-spoken and a little opinionated ("Screenshots deserve better"),
never salesy or hypey. It shows craft rather than claiming it: the site itself is a
proof-of-work for the product. The emotional goal is *quiet confidence* — the visitor
should feel they've found a serious tool made by people who care about detail, and
feel calm about privacy and cost rather than pressured.

## Anti-references

- **Generic AI/SaaS template.** No purple-gradient hero, stock glassy feature cards,
  floating-blob backgrounds, or "modern startup" boilerplate that signals
  AI-generated sameness. Distinctiveness and real product imagery over template chrome.
- **Cluttered pro-tool density (Photoshop / pro-video editors).** Don't intimidate
  with wall-to-wall toolbars, tiny dense controls, or a steep-learning-curve aesthetic.
  Approachable, not daunting.
- **Childish / cartoonish consumer cuteness.** No sticker-pile, bubbly rounded-blob,
  emoji-soup, toy-like styling. Stay refined and editorial.

## Design Principles

1. **Beautiful by default.** The result should look intentional the instant an image
   lands — sensible padding, framing, and color come for free. Never make polish
   depend on expertise or fiddling.
2. **Practice what you preach.** A tool for making beautiful screenshots must itself be
   impeccably designed. Every page and panel is a portfolio piece; craft *is* the pitch.
3. **Show, don't tell.** Let real product visuals, live demos, and actual output carry
   the argument. Earn claims with evidence, not adjectives.
4. **Privacy and openness are features, not footnotes.** Local-first rendering, free,
   and open source are core selling points — communicate the trust they buy, plainly
   and prominently.
5. **One scene, every output.** Reinforce that a single composition becomes stills,
   social sizes, store sets, GIFs, and video. Compose once, ship everywhere.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal conformance target specified. Follow general good practice: maintain legible
contrast on the dark navy palette (especially `--ink-2`/`--ink-3` secondary text over
`--bg`), keep interactive elements keyboard-reachable with visible focus, and honor
`prefers-reduced-motion` for the ambient/aurora and scroll-driven animations.
